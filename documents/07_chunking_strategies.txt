Chunking Strategies for RAG — Course Notes

Chunking is the step that splits documents into passages before embedding. It matters because retrieval works at chunk level: if a chunk is too large it mixes several topics and its embedding becomes blurry; if it is too small it loses the surrounding context the model needs to answer well.

A good starting point is paragraph-based chunking: split on blank lines, then greedily merge consecutive paragraphs until a size limit of roughly 500 to 1500 characters is reached. This keeps sentences intact and respects the author's own topic boundaries.

For long, structured documents, heading-based chunking usually beats plain paragraph merging. Splitting at section headings keeps each chunk about a single topic, and prepending the heading text to the chunk gives the embedding extra topical signal.

Chunk overlap is another common refinement: neighboring chunks share the last one or two sentences, so information sitting on a chunk boundary is not lost to retrieval. Typical overlap is 10 to 20 percent of the chunk size.

Whatever strategy is used, store the source document name and the chunk index alongside each chunk. This enables source citations in answers and makes retrieval results easy to debug.
